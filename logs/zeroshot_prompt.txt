## Research idea

```yaml
idea:
  title: LLMs are bad at keeping obvious secrets
  domain: artificial_intelligence
  hypothesis: |-
    LLMs are bad at keeping obvious secrets. For instance, when LLMs write
    fiction they foreshadow plot way too much because planning and prose
    generation are entangled: the model can't help but hint at what it knows is
    coming. The model wants to say what it's thinking, without being told it
    has to. Is this still true when it can rely on a plan it's conditioning on
    that sets up the future so it doesn't have to constantly get ready for
    that future on its own?

    Concretely: when a model writes a story while holding a secret it was told
    not to reveal, does giving it an explicit plan or outline to follow reduce
    how much of the secret leaks into the text? And what is the mechanism of
    the leak: is the secret concept active in the model's internal state
    throughout generation, and does removing it remove the leakage?
  background:
    description: |-
      What is known. The idea's author has since published the behavioural core:
      Holtzman and West, "Can You Keep a Secret? Involuntary Information
      Leakage in Language Model Writing" (arXiv 2605.10794, May 2026). A writer
      model gets a secret word plus an instruction not to mention or hint at
      it, and writes a story of about 450 words; a guesser model then picks
      which of two texts was written with the secret (two-alternative forced
      choice). They report 70-79% discrimination for several frontier models
      (two were below chance), that an "actively hide" instruction pushes
      leakage below chance, that short jokes do not leak, and that a decoy word
      redirects the leakage. That work is behavioural and API-based, and the
      paper itself says a more mechanistic study is needed. It used 15 secret
      words: umbrella, lighthouse, violin, cactus, telescope, justice,
      patience, entropy, nostalgia, freedom, bracket, Tuesday, copper, margin,
      invoice. Related lines: semantic leakage (Gonen et al. 2025); Taboo
      model organisms where a secret word is fine-tuned in and recovered with
      logit lens and SAEs (Cywinski et al.), which does not address
      involuntary leakage into unrelated text; secrecy without private state
      (Baldelli et al.); ironic negation (Mann et al.); and evidence that
      models plan future tokens in hidden states (2502.06258, 2404.00859, and
      Anthropic's planning-in-poems result).

      Gap. Two things appear untouched. (1) The idea's actual question: does
      conditioning on an explicit plan or outline reduce leakage, and does it
      hold for plot-level secrets (a twist the model knows is coming) rather
      than only single words? (2) Mechanism: is the secret represented in the
      residual stream throughout story generation, does its strength predict
      leakage, and does ablating it remove leakage? A run that only replicates
      the published secret-word effect adds little.

      Scope and pitfalls.
      - Model-size threshold (the main risk). The prior paper reports NO
        leakage for Llama 3.2-3B and Llama 3.1-8B, marginal leakage for Gemma 3
        4B (59%), and strong leakage (83-84%) for Gemma 3 12B/27B and Llama 3.3
        70B. A writer model of about 8-9B or smaller is therefore expected to
        show a floor effect with nothing to explain. Gemma 3 12B is the
        smallest model reported to leak strongly.
      - Models: gated repos (google/gemma-*, meta-llama/*) need the HF_TOKEN
        environment variable to have been granted access; if a download is
        refused, unsloth/gemma-3-12b-it works without it. SAEs for this model
        are in google/gemma-scope-2-12b-it (ungated; newer and less supported
        tooling). An OpenRouter API key is available as OPENROUTER_KEY (for
        example for the guesser model).
      - Confound in any plan condition: an outline both adds context (which
        may just dilute the secret's salience) and fixes content decisions. A
        comparison is only informative if it can tell these apart, for example
        against length-matched irrelevant context.
      - The known effects give yardsticks: the plain "secret plus do not
        reveal" condition is a positive anchor, and the decoy word is a known
        mitigation to compare effect sizes against.
      - Measurement: guesser judgments are noisy and position-biased, so both
        presentation orders and enough trials are needed to separate, say, 80%
        from 65%. For plot-level secrets, candidate twists may be guessable
        from the premise alone, so a no-secret control is needed. For
        ablations, compare against random and other-concept directions and
        check that story quality is not simply degraded.
    papers:
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10794
      description: Holtzman and West, Can You Keep a Secret? Involuntary Information Leakage in Language
        Model Writing (May 2026). The behavioural core of this idea, by its author; does not test a plan/outline
        condition, plot-level secrets, or mechanism.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14352
      description: Cywinski et al., Towards eliciting latent knowledge from LLMs with mechanistic interpretability.
        Taboo model organisms (fine-tuned Gemma-2-9b); logit lens and SAEs recover the secret word.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01070
      description: Eliciting Secret Knowledge from Language Models. Taboo-style secret elicitation; the
        secret is fine-tuned in and the model is meant to hint.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06973
      description: 'Baldelli et al., LLMs Can''t Play Hangman: On the Necessity of a Private Working Memory
        for Language Agents. Impossibility result on secrecy plus consistency without private state.'
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25187
      description: Probing the Lack of Stable Internal Beliefs in LLMs (20-questions implicit consistency).
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12381
      description: 'Mann et al., Don''t think of the white bear: ironic negation in transformer models.'
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17884
      description: Mireshghallah et al., Can LLMs Keep a Secret? Contextual-integrity view of secret keeping.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06258
      description: Emergent Response Planning in LLMs. Planning information in hidden states.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00859
      description: Do Language Models Plan Ahead for Future Tokens?
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17119
      description: Suspense generation with outlines; evaluates suspense, not leakage.
```
